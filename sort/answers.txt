sort1 uses: Bubble Sort

How do you know?: The reversed text took the longest compared to random and sorted as Bubble Sort repeatedly compares adjacent elements and swaps them when they are in the wrong order. A reversed list requires many swaps, whereas an already sorted list requires very few.

sort2 uses: Merge Sort

How do you know?: It was consistently the fastest of the three sorting algorithms. Merge Sort divides the input into smaller parts and merges them back together efficiently, resulting in O(n log n) time complexity.

sort3 uses: Selection Sort

How do you know?: It was consistently slower than Merge Sort even when the list is sorted. Selection Sort repeatedly searches the unsorted portion for the smallest element, resulting in O(n²) time complexity.
